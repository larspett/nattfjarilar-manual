# Setting out traps: general principles

This applies to the gradient part of the project (see [Grid](rutnat-lund-uppsala.md) for that part).

## Choosing trap sites

At your site, choose **4 trap positions**:

- As similar to each other as possible: either the same habitat (e.g. all in coniferous forest) or a similar mix of habitats at all four positions
- Preferably at least **50 metres** between each trap
- All positions should be "as similar to each other, and as good, as possible" based on your judgement. The idea is that none of the positions should be better than the others, and that the position itself shouldn't affect the light trap's effectiveness

![Illustration of four trap positions (A–D) in a semi-open pasture landscape, with a 50 m scale bar](../../assets/images/fallplatser-oversikt.png)

## Drawing lots for trap type

Once the positions are chosen, draw lots for which of the four trap types (see [Trap types](../falltyper/oversikt.md)) goes at which position.

**Important**: that assignment then applies for the whole season. It's essential to the study design that trap types aren't swapped between positions during the course of the experiment, even if it's tempting to try a different trap at a different spot. You're free to experiment with the traps outside of the regular weekly sampling, but the actual sampling rounds must use the fixed positions.

## Documenting the site

During **a dedicated campaign in August 2026** (exact dates to be announced) we'll document every trap position with a standardised set of photos. This documentation is done once and doesn't need to be repeated unless the site changes dramatically.

The approach is adapted from [LUCAS — Land Use/Cover Area frame Survey](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Glossary:Land_use/cover_area_frame_survey_(LUCAS)), the EU's standardised land-cover survey. The [LUCAS methodology](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=LUCAS_-_Land_use_and_land_cover_survey#:~:text=of%20landscape%20fragmentation.-,The%20LUCAS%20survey,by%20surveyors%20on%20the%20ground.) prescribes a similar set of photos at every sample point, to enable systematic comparisons of environments.

### How to take the photos

![Illustration from above: person photographing eastward with phone in landscape orientation, compass rose and trap label](../../assets/images/habitatdok-ovanifraan.png)

Hold the phone in **landscape orientation** for all directional photos. Aim so that roughly **one-sixth of the image is sky** — in other words, the horizon should be fairly close to the top of the frame. Use your phone's compass function to find the right direction.

Take the following **six photos at every trap position**:

| Photo | Subject | Instruction |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The trap from the side | About 5 m away, trap centred in the frame |
| 2 | Straight up (canopy cover) | Hold the phone horizontal with the lens pointing straight up at the sky |
| 3 | North | Stand at the trap, face north, roughly 1/6 sky |
| 4 | East | Stand at the trap, face east, roughly 1/6 sky |
| 5 | South | Stand at the trap, face south, roughly 1/6 sky |
| 6 | West | Stand at the trap, face west, roughly 1/6 sky |

![Example of the six photo directions: trap (view), sky (straight up), north, east, south, west](../../assets/images/habitatdok-foton.png)

The coordinates for each trap position are captured automatically from the app when you register it, and don't need to be noted separately (see [Register your trap](../../hur-du-rapporterar/registrera-falla.md)).

Curious what this type of photo can show at a landscape level? The [LUCAS Statistical Atlas](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistical-atlas/?config=LUCAS) gives an interactive view of land data and land cover across the whole EU.
